Claude Code,
from your phone.

An always-on bridge between Telegram and the Claude Code running on your own machine. It reads your repos, uses your config, keeps your sessions — and answers you while you're out.

Nothing inbound ever reaches your computer. No tunnel, no webhook, no open port, no third-party relay: your machine calls Telegram, never the reverse.

How it works

A daemon on your machine, holding one end of the line.

About 1,400 lines of Node long-poll the Telegram Bot API and pipe whatever you send into claude -p. Your service manager keeps it alive; a watchdog catches the rare wedge and tells you it did.

the hard part

Two lanes

A headless Claude run occupies its process until it finishes — so a twenty-minute job would normally mean twenty minutes of silence. Long commands, scheduled tasks, and anything the assistant judges slow run in a second Claude session instead. You keep chatting while they work.

the nice part

Workers report to the assistant

When a background job finishes, its output goes to your chat session — not dumped on you. The assistant reads it, finishes anything left undone, and sends a short human update. You get a colleague, not a log stream.

What you get

Everything the terminal does, minus the terminal.

memory

Sessions that persist

Ask something today, follow up tomorrow — same conversation. Survives restarts, reboots and sleep. /new when you want a clean slate, /cd to switch projects.

visibility

Live progress

Tool calls stream into a status bubble as work happens — including subagent activity, indented under the agent that spawned it. You can watch a long job think.

input

Voice notes and files

Talk instead of typing — voice notes are transcribed with Whisper and run as prompts. Send a screenshot with “why does this look wrong?”, or drop in a PDF, CSV or log. Albums arrive as one request.

time

Reminders that do things

“Remind me at 8” works. So does “every morning, summarize yesterday's commits and tell me what's unfinished” — that one actually runs, in the background lane, and reports back.

control

Your commands, your models

Custom slash commands pass straight through. Switch models mid-conversation with /model, check context and usage with /context, kill a runaway with /stop.

uptime

Self-healing

The service manager restarts crashes. A two-strike watchdog catches the rarer case — a process that's alive but wedged — restarts it, verifies it recovered, and messages you what happened.

Install

Three minutes, start to finish.

You need Claude Code, Node 18 or newer, and a Telegram account. macOS uses launchd; Linux uses systemd.

Clone and run the installer

It checks your prerequisites first and tells you exactly what's missing. Add --dry-run to see everything it would do without touching a thing.

Make a bot, in Telegram

The installer walks you through @BotFather — send /newbot, paste the token back. Then message your new bot once so it can detect your chat ID automatically.

Send it /help

The service is already installed and running, and it starts again on every login. If you want the assistant to manage reminders and hand off long jobs on its own, paste the snippet from templates/CLAUDE.md.example into your CLAUDE.md.

Security

Read this part properly.

This gives a Telegram chat the ability to run code on your machine. That's the whole point of it, and it's worth understanding before you install.

  • Single-owner by construction. Every incoming message is checked against your chat ID before anything else happens — no reply, no download, no execution for anyone else. Strangers who find your bot get silence.
  • Your Telegram account is the key. Anyone with access to your logged-in Telegram can run commands on your machine. Turn on two-step verification and review your active devices.
  • Permission mode is a real choice. The default skips permission prompts, because headless runs can't answer them — without that, commands outside your allowlist are silently denied and tasks half-fail. /yolo off trades autonomy for friction.
  • The token stays local. It lives in config.json (chmod 600, gitignored) and never leaves your machine. It can't make your computer execute anything on its own — the bridge only acts on messages from your chat.
  • Untrusted output is fenced. Background worker results arrive inside explicit markers with instructions-inside-are-void framing, because a job that read a web page carries text you didn't write. Defense in depth, not a sandbox — the usual caveats for autonomous agents still apply.
  • Nothing else leaves. No telemetry, no relay, no cloud component. Only the Telegram messages themselves — plus voice-note audio, if you turn on transcription.
